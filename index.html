Newton's Law of Cooling — Virtual Lab Simulation

1. Theory & Background

Aim

To verify Newton's Law of Cooling by plotting a cooling curve of a given liquid sample and determining the cooling constant $k$.

Apparatus Required

  • Double-walled calorimeter with wooden base
  • Conical boiler flask with electric immersion heater
  • Precision thermometer (0.1 deg C graduation)
  • Connecting rubber tubing for vapor/heat transfer
  • Stopwatch / Digital timer
  • Liquid sample (Water / Kerosene Oil / Alcohol)
  • Solid material blocks (Brass, Copper, Aluminium, Silver, Iron)

Theory

Newton's Law of Cooling states that the rate of loss of heat of a liquid body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings, provided the temperature difference is small.

-dT / dt = k (T - Ts)

Integrating: T(t) = Ts + (T0 - Ts) * exp(-k * t)

  • T = Temperature of the body at time t
  • T0 = Initial temperature of the body
  • Ts = Temperature of the surrounding medium (Ambient Temp)
  • k = Characteristic cooling constant (min^-1) depending on surface area and material properties

Application

Used in metallurgy for casting cooling rates, engine block thermal dissipation studies, meteorological forecasting, and forensic body temperature estimation post-mortem.

Basic Instructions

Ensure draught-free surrounding conditions, proper calibration of the thermometer, and uniform stirring of the liquid sample during real laboratory execution.

2. Experimental Procedure & Guidelines

User Instructions

Familiarize yourself with the control panels on the right side of the simulator before initiating heating cycles.

Variable Menu

Select your desired calorimeter material, liquid medium, initial temperature T0, and ambient temperature Ts from the interactive dropdowns and sliders.

Measurement Menu

Monitor real-time thermometer readings, elapsed time counters, and thermal gradients (T - Ts) directly from the instrumentation readout box.

Procedure for Real Lab

  1. Fill the double-walled calorimeter with the given hot liquid sample up to the designated mark.
  2. Insert the thermometer and stirrer through the insulated wooden lid.
  3. Note the initial temperature T0 and start the stopwatch simultaneously.
  4. Record temperature readings at regular time intervals (e.g., every 1 or 2 minutes) until the liquid cools significantly.
  5. Measure the steady surrounding room temperature Ts.
